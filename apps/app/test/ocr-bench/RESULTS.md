# OCR benchmark results

Generated by `test/ocr-bench/report.mjs` from `results/*.json`. 30 synthetic Cameroonian documents (ENEO / CAMWATER bills with tables,
school-fee notices, hospital appointment letters, MoMo SMS screenshots; French and English), degraded with perspective tilt up to 8 deg,
rotation up to 5 deg, desk background, uneven light, creases, low resolution, blur, noise and JPEG artefacts (two shots are turned 90 / 180 deg).
Tesseract.js 7 in Node, fra+eng, same traineddata as the app (`public/ocr`).

**Fact accuracy** = dates, FCFA amounts and phone numbers that `@sabitori/core` `extractFacts` finds in the OCR text, compared with the facts it
finds in the ground-truth text (so the metric measures what OCR noise destroys, not extractor coverage). Recall = share of true facts recovered;
precision = share of reported facts that are true; a wrong digit is both a miss and a false fact. CER = character error rate of the whole page.
The default pipeline was chosen by fact F1, not CER.

## Shipped now: table rules erased (2026-11-17)

School-fee notices were the weakest document type: Tesseract skipped the text inside their ruled tables (deadlines and instalment amounts), even on
clean photos. `eraseRules` in `src/lib/ocr-prep.ts` now turns long, thin, straight ink runs with paper on both sides into paper, only when the page
has rules both ways (a table); a lone letterhead line is left alone. Same 30 pages, shipped pipeline with the capped half-turn retry, 3 workers
(`OCR_BENCH=rules`):

| Variant | CER % | Fact recall % | Fact precision % | Fact F1 % | Pages with every fact right | Prep ms | OCR ms | Total ms (max) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| before: no rule erasing (capped retry) | 25.7 | 75.0 | 97.2 | 84.7 | 16/30 | 232 | 3871 | 4103 (11100) |
| shipped: erase table rules (capped retry) | 23.6 | 84.3 | 97.5 | 90.4 | 20/30 | 299 | 3889 | 4188 (10824) |

| Document | eneo | camwater | school | hospital | momo |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Before (recall %) | 90 | 73 | 33 | 88 | 100 |
| Shipped (recall %) | 93 | 73 | 67 | 88 | 100 |

No document type lost facts; school notices went from a third to about two thirds of their facts. Since 2026-11-19 the
fact reader also reads an OCR-garbled month between a day and a year ("27 noveribwe 2026"; `nearMonth` in
`packages/core/src/facts.ts`): shipped recall 83.6% -> 84.3% (school 64% -> 67%), precision unchanged. Timing columns are from
the first run. The tables below were measured before this
step and are kept as the record of how the pipeline was chosen.

## Which pipeline steps earn their place (cumulative, run before the final fixes: no invert step, no half-turn retry)

| Variant | CER % | Fact recall % | Fact precision % | Fact F1 % | Pages with every fact right | Prep ms | OCR ms | Total ms (max) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A legacy (grey+contrast) | 28.0 | 62.9 | 91.7 | 74.6 | 11/30 | 9 | 1520 | 1529 (3258) |
| B grey only | 28.4 | 63.6 | 89.0 | 74.2 | 11/30 | 3 | 1315 | 1318 (2999) |
| C +normalise | 64.8 | 63.6 | 91.8 | 75.1 | 11/30 | 77 | 4054 | 4130 (8167) |
| D +crop | 40.8 | 66.4 | 93.0 | 77.5 | 15/30 | 79 | 2128 | 2207 (7117) |
| E +orient | 40.7 | 66.4 | 92.1 | 77.2 | 15/30 | 153 | 2157 | 2310 (6987) |
| F +deskew | 38.9 | 64.3 | 90.9 | 75.3 | 13/30 | 167 | 2031 | 2198 (7753) |
| G +scale | 36.3 | 69.3 | 95.1 | 80.2 | 15/30 | 227 | 2396 | 2624 (5121) |
| H +sauvola | 46.4 | 60.7 | 92.4 | 73.3 | 11/30 | 277 | 2655 | 2932 (6602) |

Reading it: normalisation alone does not help unless the desk is cropped away first (Tesseract wastes time and invents text on the background);
Sauvola binarisation hurts (it throws away the grey edge information the LSTM uses) so it stays available but off; upscaling small text helps both recall
and precision.
Deskew is neutral within the noise of a 30-page set (E to F is -1.9 F1 here, +0.8 in the tuning table T5 vs T2); it is kept because tilted pages are the stated problem and the unit tests verify it recovers known angles to under 0.8 deg. A sampling bug in the skew estimator (stride aliasing with the projection bins) was found by the unit tests and fixed before these runs.

## Model files and core variants (app size)

Same 30 pages, shipped pipeline (default prep + half-turn retry), fra+eng, 3 parallel workers. Only the traineddata differs
(`OCR_BENCH=models`, see `variants.ts` for the env vars that point at each folder):

| Variant | CER % | Fact recall % | Fact precision % | Fact F1 % | Pages with every fact right | Prep ms | OCR ms | Total ms (max) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| best float (4.0.0) | 25.7 | 75.0 | 94.6 | 83.7 | 16/30 | 260 | 5283 | 5543 (14704) |
| best_int (4.0.0_best_int) | 25.7 | 75.0 | 94.6 | 83.7 | 16/30 | 262 | 4439 | 4702 (13894) |
| fast (tessdata_fast) | 26.7 | 72.1 | 94.4 | 81.8 | 15/30 | 236 | 3976 | 4213 (11307) |

| Traineddata (fra + eng, gzip) | Size | Fact F1 vs `best float` |
|---|---|---|
| best float, `@tesseract.js-data/*/4.0.0` (what the app shipped: tessdata_best) | 17.2 MB | 0 |
| **best_int, `@tesseract.js-data/*/4.0.0_best_int` (integer-quantised tessdata_best) -- shipped now** | **3.7 MB** | **0.0 points** (same recall, precision and CER to 0.1; 15 of 30 pages read byte-identical, the rest differ by a character or two) |
| fast, tessdata_fast (Apache-2.0) | 2.6 MB | -1.9 points (recall 75.0 to 72.1) |

The rule was: switch only if fact F1 drops by 1 point or less. `fast` loses 1.9, so it is **not** used. `best_int` loses nothing on this set, is
already inside the `@tesseract.js-data/*` packages the app depends on (no new dependency, no download step), and saves 13.5 MB, so the app ships it
(`apps/app/vite.config.ts` copies `4.0.0_best_int/<lang>.traineddata.gz`). Same OCR time or slightly less (4.4 s vs 5.3 s per page here). The set is
synthetic and small (30 pages), so re-run the `models` set if the page set changes.

**Tesseract core variants.** tesseract.js picks one of three LSTM-only cores at run time (non-SIMD, SIMD, relaxed SIMD, 3.9 MB each). Measured in Node 22, five of
the benchmark photos, fra+eng, best_int, one worker:

| Core | Time for 5 pages |
|---|---|
| relaxed SIMD | 4.8 s |
| SIMD | 4.8 s (4.9 s on a second run) |
| no SIMD | 15.5 s (14.9 s on a second run) |

Relaxed SIMD is not faster for this model, so it is not shipped. The non-SIMD core is 3x slower and is only needed by an Android System WebView older than 91
(May 2021), which lacks WebAssembly SIMD. Decision: **ship only `tesseract-core-simd-lstm.wasm.js`.** Reasons: the app's onnxruntime-web build (encoder and Piper) already needs WebAssembly SIMD, so
on such an old WebView the app is degraded anyway (rules instead of the encoder, phone voice instead of Piper); Android System WebView is updated through Google Play, and
the 3.9 MB is paid by every user to cover a shrinking minority. The minSdk is 24 and no minimum WebView version is enforced by the app, so this is a judgement, not a
guarantee. `ocr.ts` names
the SIMD core explicitly (a `.js` `corePath` bypasses the loader's own detection, which would otherwise ask for a file that is no longer there on a
phone with relaxed SIMD) and checks SIMD support first, so a WebView without it gets a clear error instead of a failed download. Cost of the decision:
on a phone without SIMD, photo reading (OCR) stops working; pasted and shared text still works through the rules. To bring that case back, copy
`tesseract-core-lstm.wasm.js` as well in `vite.config.ts` and choose the file in `ocr.ts` by the same SIMD check (+3.9 MB).

## Review 3: dark-mode detection and the half-turn retry

Dark-mode detection (`isLightOnDark`) now measures the working-size image instead of a 200 px thumbnail, which averaged the thin light strokes
of a dark SMS screenshot away (a reviewer's screenshot: 99.8th percentile 238 at full size, under 130 at 200 px, so it was never inverted and the
phone number was lost). The half-turn retry now runs only when the first pass is very unsure (confidence under 40), or unsure (under 55) and found
fewer than 2 facts. Same 30 pages, one worker at a time (`OCR_BENCH=review3 OCR_PAR=1`); both rows use the new detection:

| Variant | CER % | Fact recall % | Fact precision % | Fact F1 % | Pages with every fact right | Prep ms | OCR ms | Total ms (max) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| default fra+eng +retry (every page < 55) | 25.7 | 75.0 | 97.2 | 84.7 | 16/30 | 266 | 3943 | 4209 (12353) |
| default fra+eng +capped retry (shipped) | 25.7 | 75.0 | 97.2 | 84.7 | 16/30 | 286 | 3543 | 3829 (11763) |

No loss of fact F1 against the rule before (every page under 55); the mean OCR time per page drops because confidently-read facts are no longer re-read
upside down. The F1 here is 1 point above the "final" table because fact extraction gives fewer false facts on three ENEO pages than when that table was made (same OCR text, no page was inverted differently); compare rows within a table.

The reviewer's SMS screenshots (light, dark with a message bubble, dark without; `OCR_BENCH=screens OCR_BENCH_DIR=screens`), with and without the invert step:

## Chosen defaults

`PREP_DEFAULT`: invert (dark-mode screenshots, detected at working size) on, crop on, illumination normalisation on, sideways detection on, deskew (+-15 deg) on, rescale to 34 px
text lines (max 3x up, 2000 px longest side) on, table rules erased (tables only) on, Sauvola off. Language fra+eng. Half-turn retry below 40 % confidence, or below 55 % when the first
pass found fewer than 2 facts.

## Known limits

- Perspective is not corrected (only rotation); strong tilt still lowers accuracy on the harshest pages.
- 180-degree orientation: ink statistics proved unreliable (tested centroid/ascender bias: false flips on upright pages), and Tesseract OSD data (`osd.traineddata`) is not shipped offline, so a low-confidence half-turn retry is used instead.
- The existing sharpness check (Laplacian variance) is inflated by sensor noise: every benchmark photo, including noisy ones, passes it. Not changed here.
- The synthetic set is only a proxy; results on real photos will differ. Images are regenerated with `OCR_BENCH_GEN=1` (see `gen.test.ts`); the benchmark runs with `OCR_BENCH=<set>` (see `variants.ts`).
